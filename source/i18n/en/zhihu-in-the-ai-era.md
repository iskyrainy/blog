Today is the second day of the 2026 college entrance examination, and it has been exactly eight years since I finished mine. I first learned about Zhihu during my high school years—I remember it clearly. I was quite envious that my deskmate had his own phone, so that after school he could read all kinds of novels and magazines to his heart's content. My deskmate frequently browsed the Zhihu community and would even print out his favorite answers, eventually accumulating a thick volume. The one that left the deepest impression on me was an answer about cell towers, filled with pictures and explanations of various cell towers/disguised cell towers (to protect against electromagnetic radiation, 😇). That answer reshaped the worldview of a small-town high school student like me, who had been instilled since childhood with the idea of just studying hard and nothing else. I was amazed that someone *could* have such deep knowledge and research on things beyond studying, and that was when I noted down the name Zhihu.

It wasn't until after the 2018 college entrance exam that I got my first phone. Since then, I've frequently browsed all kinds of topics on Zhihu and the answers from all sorts of people—they come from every corner of the country, have different professions and different lives, and hold different views on the same topic. "Among any three people walking, I will find something to learn from." The collision of different viewpoints also subtly changed me, and this is what I consider the value of Zhihu. Among all the sections on Zhihu, the most valuable is undoubtedly the technology section. Zhihu's technology section has many experts from both industry and academia, and the tech blogs of some major internet companies are also posted on Zhihu. These articles or answers range from introductory popular science to in-depth and specialized sharing, and every time I read these high-quality words, they bring me immense spiritual pleasure. Or rather, any writing into which someone has poured their heart, no matter the section, is a joy to read. As the saying goes, one leaf reveals the coming of autumn—through reading the words you can experience the author's thought process, and different experiences and different ideas broaden one's horizons and keep one's thinking from being confined. If only it could stay like this forever.

Nothing in the world is static. I am no longer the child I was eight years ago, and the times are no longer the booming internet era of back then. Eight years have changed a lot—I lived through the pandemic and then caught the explosive growth of LLMs. Now, 2026 is the era of AI, and everything is moving toward AI, whether products or technology. The use of AI tools has produced a massive amount of AI content, and now every community is flooded with AI-generated content, most of which is frankly hard to describe. I am not opposed to using AI or AI-generated content—I also use various AIs extensively to complete work or learn about knowledge, including some of my previous blog posts that I compiled after consulting AI, understanding, and revising. What I oppose is AI content filled with strange metaphors, endless nonsense, and superficiality. These articles are occupied for most of their length by too much filler, and reading these low-density words means the brain, while thinking, must also constantly filter out useless information and convert abstract information—it is just too, too exhausting. Some scenarios that could easily be explained with pseudocode instead have to invent a Little Red and a Little Ming for a metaphor—is that really good?

In essence, AI is a tool that generates content based on probability prediction. AI can also generate high-quality articles, but it requires the author to carefully craft prompts and to understand and digest the AI content themselves. I believe that if the author themselves can immerse themselves in and enjoy reading AI content, then does it really matter whether such high-quality writing is produced by AI? After the pandemic, the community environment is already far inferior to before. After some people who were devoted to different specialized fields left, the quality of Zhihu's content is no longer what it once was. With the impact of the AI era, eight out of ten tech posts in recommendations are crudely made AI articles. Ah, perhaps it's hard for others to appreciate how utterly exhausting it is to scroll past such a piece of AI-generated garbage right after waking up in the morning or after a tiring day at work.

I hope Zhihu can scan answers and articles in advance, identify whether they are AI-created, and let users independently set the proportion of AI-created content in their feeds; give AI-created content an independent rating; and let AI-created content have its core viewpoints and content summarized and distilled by AI (AI against AI, 😇). I also hope Zhihu gets better and better. 🤠

> In memory of the good times Zhihu brought me.

Another year has passed without my noticing. This is my first time writing a year-end review, and only when I wanted to start did I realize that time is growing thinner and thinner. I really like that "logarithmic life" theory—in the first year after birth, that year accounts for one hundred percent of your life, but each subsequent year is just a smaller and smaller fraction of it.

When I began to seriously look back on this year, I found I didn't know what to write. I suddenly regretted not taking more photos—at least with photos I could briefly relive the past. People always say, "What you can't remember doesn't matter," but do people really wish for so many unforgettable memories? Perhaps it's not that "what you can't remember doesn't matter," but rather that "everything matters, but you can't remember it all"—which is why we need to write something down to record it.

Last year during the Spring Festival, unusually, there was no snow; the first snowfall only came around the Lantern Festival. In recent years, temperatures have been getting higher and higher—scorching summers, abundant rainfall, and the precipitation belt shifting northward. Perhaps the Qinling-Huaihe line will no longer be the north-south dividing line in the future. Still, Henan and Yujun—in ancient times, this was already a warm region.

## Goals and Procrastination
I set a whole pile of goals over the year, most of which were carried out at a discount:
- Planned to read more books, ended up reading three and a half;
- Wanted to ride my bike more, but spring and autumn slipped by in an instant;
- Wanted to work on my own projects, but the final stretch of two projects dragged on for a long time;
- Also wanted to write three blog posts a month, but in reality I wrote fewer and fewer as the year went on;
...

It seems like there was plenty of time, yet it passed much faster than I imagined. I kept telling myself, "I'll do it later," "Tomorrow," "There's still time." I really envy those people with strong self-control who mean what they say and say what they mean—perhaps their lives are perfectly fitted together, meticulous and precise.

## Gains and Happiness
> Aim high, and you may achieve the middle; aim for the middle, and you may achieve the low; aim for the low, and you shall achieve nothing at all. -- *The Analects · Zi Han*

Though I procrastinated, I was fortunate that I set my goals high—even if I only reached part of them, I still gained something.

### Reading

> *I read your poems by lamplight before the lamp; the poems end, the lamp dims, and dawn has not yet come.*

*High Performance MySQL* helped me understand MySQL's underlying architecture, so that when designing database tables and writing SQL, I think from a deeper perspective;

*The Rust Programming Language* introduced me to one of the most modern and interesting languages, and how it cleverly guarantees *memory safe* at the compiler stage;

*Tokio in Rust* let me learn the finest and most popular Rust asynchronous runtime, and dive deep into *I/O Multiplexing*;
